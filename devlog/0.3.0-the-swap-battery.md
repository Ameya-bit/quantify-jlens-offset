## What step 6 asked

H3: is the offset an artefact of the transport step — the matrix that
carries an early-layer activation to layer 30? The lever is a swap: J and R
are two different recipes for that matrix (R is built via LRP, a modified
backward pass) sharing everything else — model, final norm, unembedding —
so scores land on one comparable scale. Registered dichotomy (steps.md, set
19 Aug): offset SHRINKS under the swap ⇒ transport error, decisive;
SURVIVES ⇒ underdetermined (not-transport vs both-recipes-share-the-error).

Offset size, per instrument and layer (D39, bands set before any ratio was
computed): median m_t of the top-100 tokens by m_t minus the vocabulary
median, in logit units. Ratio R/J per layer; <0.7 shrinks, 0.7–1.3
survives, >1.3 grows. Verdict on the early band L0–4 where the junk lives.

## Finding 1 — the early offset GROWS under the swap and changes identity

Outside the registered dichotomy entirely. Early-band mean R/J = **1.94**
(K=100; per-layer 1.71–2.26 over L0–9, K=1000 matches, split halves agree
to ±0.03). And the two offsets are made of **different tokens**: top-100
overlap J∩R is 0.03–0.14 at L0–5 against a split-half noise ceiling of
0.86–0.98 — real divergence, not tail noise. Composition differs by eye:
J's early top-100 is `\xa0`/soft-hyphen/byte-fragment/code debris (junk
share 0.22, never-seen-in-pile 0.35); R's is the D30 web-debris axis
(` somew`, ` whats`, ` diety`, ` atleast`, `…but`, `–and`) plus CJK
fragments (junk 0.16, never-seen 0.54). Both carry the multiplicative
signature (top-100 at the 95th–98th sigma_t percentile).

Reading: the differing part of the early offset cannot come from the shared
parts — each recipe manufactures its own junk. This is the strongest pro-H3
evidence in the study, stronger than the anticipated dichotomy allowed. It
does NOT show the offset is *entirely* transport: the logit lens (no
transport) has its own nonzero offset, and a shared component clearly
exists (Finding 2).

## Finding 2 — the shared component lives at mid-depth and is the frequency prior

From L14 the ratio settles into the survives band (1.02–1.26), J∩R overlap
rises to 0.50–0.69 against a ~0.76–0.87 ceiling, and at L30 ratio = 1.0000,
overlap = 1.00 exactly (J₃₀=R₃₀=I — the machinery self-validates). The 4c
frequency correlations transfer across the swap: max |J−R| Spearman gap
0.17 (latin_pile), 0.13 (nonlatin_wordfreq) over all layers. Depth
decomposition of the offset: early = recipe-specific transport artefact;
mid = shared frequency-shaped component (the registered H1, which survives
the swap); L30 baseline = the model's true average-logit spread (offset
size 2.25 for all three instruments there — absolute sizes are NOT error
magnitudes; the ratio and the membership carry the finding).

## Finding 3 — step 5's R-only residue reinterpreted as instrument

The narrow real H2 residue (0.2.0 Finding 2) lived on the R-lens only and
was carried by informal/misspelled web-debris tokens. Those are exactly the
tokens R's transport puts in its early offset (Finding 1). The residue now
reads as a property of R's recipe, not of tuning. Stated as reinterpretation,
not proof: the step-5 numbers stand; their attribution changes.

## Finding 4 — the late junk rebound, on m_t itself

The banked step-2 readout finding re-measured on the offset: junk share of
the top-100 m_t goes 0.00–0.03 (L18–21) → 0.61–0.70 (L27–28) on J, same on
R, stable across halves. Caveat the step-2 version did not need: on m_t the
LOGIT lens is not monotone-clean — it is junky through mid-depth
(0.14–0.42 at L18–21) and flatter late (0.43–0.62). The sharp mid→late
rebound remains a transported-instrument signature; "junk only in the
translated instruments" does not carry over to m_t and is not claimed.

## Bias–variance signature (why a "better" lens has a bigger offset)

R's m_t is more repeatable than J's at 31/31 layers (split-half Pearson;
30/31 by top-100 stability) while its constant offset is ~2× larger early:
lower variance, higher bias — the signature of a regularised estimator.
Consistent with the R-lens post's accuracy claim: a constant offset is the
most subtractable kind of error, so R can be both more biased (here) and
more accurate after correction (their claim, and step 7's testable
prediction: the m_t correction should work best on R).

## Verdict against the registered H3

Early-layer offset: transport-recipe-specific in both size and membership —
H3 SUPPORTED early, with the "shared error" escape hatch closed by the
divergence itself. Mid-depth offset: survives the swap, frequency-shaped —
NOT transport; the registered H1 component. No tension with Anthropic's
"no R-lens advantage at 4B": that claim concerns readout accuracy, not
offset size.

## Honest flags

1. **Soft pre-registration:** D39's bands were fixed before any ratio was
   computed, but after step 2 had shown J≈R on related junk metrics.
   GROWS was outside the registered dichotomy; the interpretation is
   post-hoc and the writeup must say so.
2. **Band means mislead:** mid-band mean ratio 1.40 mixes L5–9 (~2.0) with
   L14–28 (~1.1). The per-layer profile is the honest picture.
3. **Top-100 membership is the noisiest statistic in the project** (step
   4.0 caveat); every overlap is read against the split-half ceiling, never
   against 1.0.
4. **One instrument pair, one model.** J-vs-R on Qwen3.5-4B is a single
   contrast; "each recipe manufactures its own junk" generalises only as
   far as that.
