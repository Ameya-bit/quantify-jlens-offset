# 0.1.0 — The offset battery (step 4 complete)

*21 Aug 2026. First study-step entry (numbering: `0.x.0` = a steps.md step
done). Everything below is Qwen3.5-4B unless a model is named.*

## What step 4 asked

Every hypothesis in this project is a question about one object: **m_t**, the
mean readout score of token *t* at layer *ℓ*, averaged over many unrelated
text positions. If a lens keeps reading out the same tokens regardless of
input, that shows up as a high mean — the "offset." Step 4 had to (4.0) prove
m_t is a stable property rather than sampling noise, then characterize it
three ways: (4a) the analytic bias route on LayerNorm models, (4b) the
mean-activation route, (4c) the frequency regression.

**Sample:** 100 pile-10k documents, seed 2, drawn from rows 25+ with every
row used by the step-2 survey (seed 0) and the Δ_t sample (seed 1) excluded —
the exclusion lists are read back from those steps' results files, not
re-derived, and an assertion fails the run on any overlap. 20 random
positions ≥ 4 per document, 1,987 (document, position) samples total. All
averaging in the declared score space: pre-softmax logits (`src/lens.py`).
`src/offset_battery.py` → `results/step4_mt.npz` (m_t, σ_t per instrument ×
layer × token, plus each half's m_t) + `results/step4_noise_null.json`.

## 4.0 The noise null — GATE PASSED

Design: the 100 documents are split into two disjoint halves (by document,
never by position; 1,000 vs 987 samples), m_t is computed independently on
each, and the halves are correlated per layer. **The pass criterion was
pre-registered before any number existed (D34): split-half Pearson ≥ 0.9 at
every layer for the J-lens.**

Result: **minimum J-lens Pearson 0.9589** (layer 8), median 0.9757. R-lens
min 0.9625, logit-lens min 0.9570; Spearman ≥ 0.95 everywhere for all three.
The offset is a stable property of the model+lens pair, not of our sample.
Same design on Pythia-1.4B with our own fitted lens
(`src.offset_battery_pythia` → `results/step4_mt_pythia.npz`): **min J-lens
Pearson 0.9902**.

**Honest flag — the tail is noisier than the vector.** The top-100 *identity*
overlap between halves is only 0.56–0.84 (J median 0.69). The offset as a
248k-dim direction replicates almost perfectly; exactly which 100 tokens top
the list does not. Any claim about specific top tokens carries that noise;
claims about the vector do not.

**Honest flag — stability is not bias.** The null proves m_t is stable, not
that it is an artifact: a model that genuinely predicts frequent tokens often
also has a stable, replicating m_t. Separating "instrument bias" from "stable
content tendency" is what 4a/4b and the σ_t diagnostics are for.

## The σ_t diagnostic — the multiplicative signature is PRESENT

The plan's classification: high mean + low variance = additive offset; high
variance = content; **both elevated = the multiplicative/unembedding-norm
signature (Neel's aside), to be flagged if seen. It is seen.** The top-100
tokens by m_t sit at the **91st–99th percentile of σ_t** at every layer and
every instrument (`src.mt_diagnostics` → `results/step4_mt_diagnostics.json`).
The tokens the lens over-reads on average are also the tokens it varies most
on — scores behave as if scaled per-token, not shifted per-token. Route B's
centering result (below) lands the same way, and 4a's row-norm diagnostic
gives the mechanism a candidate: unembedding row norms are largest for rare
tokens (r = −0.21 Pythia, −0.54 GPT-2 vs log frequency).

Face validity of the composition (top-100 by m_t, examples at
`results/step4_mt_diagnostics.json`): J-lens L0 is `\xa0`, ` \xad` (soft
hyphen), `�`, code fragments — the step-2 junk exactly (junk share 0.22);
L12 is ` thru`, `...`, whitespace runs — the same "raw web debris" axis that
dominates Δ_t (D23); L24 is function words and punctuation (junk share 0.09).

## 4a Route A — phoenix REPLICATES; the bias explains the logit lens, not the J-lens

The derivation, stated once: LayerNorm ends with γ⊙norm(h) + β, so
logits = W_U·(γ⊙norm(h)) + W_U·β — an exact split into a context term and a
per-token constant computable from weights alone. RMSNorm has no β, so on
Qwen this term does not exist; that is why Pythia and GPT-2 are in the study.
`src.route_a` → `results/step4_route_a.json` + `step4_wu_beta.npz`.

**The phoenix cell: replicated.** r(W_U·β, log f_t) on GPT-2 = **0.70**
(capped pile counts; 0.70 full; Spearman 0.61) against the public claim of
r ≈ 0.67 — from weights alone, no forward passes. Pythia-1.4B: **0.73**.
Controls: five random directions of β's norm give |r| ≤ 0.29 (the null
envelope is not zero — W_U geometry alone carries frequency signal — but β
sits 2.5–3.5× outside it). Zero-count tokens excluded, share ≤ 2.3% per
number.

**The hypothesis test proper dissociates by instrument.** Correlating the
analytic constant with Pythia's *empirical* m_t: for the **logit lens** r
climbs from 0.42 (L0) to **0.93–0.95 from L12 on** — at mid/late depth the
raw logit-lens offset essentially *is* the LayerNorm bias. For the **J-lens**
r is 0.21–0.72, non-monotonic (min 0.21 at L16). **The transported lens's
offset is not mostly the bias** — whatever the J-lens adds, it comes from
somewhere else. This is the cleanest mechanism split step 4 produced.

## 4b Route B — the offset lives in the mean activation, but is not removable by subtracting it

μ̄ = the mean residual per layer, estimated from the half-0 documents only;
every centering evaluation runs on half-1 documents the estimate never saw
(out-of-sample, D35). Subtraction is applied to the activation before the
lens — never to scores. `src.route_b` → `results/step4_route_b.json` +
`step4_mu.npz`.

**Reading out μ̄ reproduces the offset almost exactly:** Spearman(s(μ̄), m_t)
= **0.98–0.997 at every layer, every instrument**, and its top tokens are the
m_t top tokens (J L0: `\xa0`, ` \xad`, `\xa0\xa0`…). Near-linearity of the
readout means mean-of-scores ≈ score-of-mean; the content of the offset is
carried by the average activation direction. This is the Route B mechanism —
and it is available on Qwen, where Route A is impossible by construction.

**But the junk does NOT disappear under centering — it increases.** Junk
fraction of top-10 readouts (non-Latin | byte rule from step 2), raw →
centered, band means: J-lens L0–10 **0.139 → 0.196**, L11–21 0.089 → 0.179,
L22–30 0.084 → 0.152; R-lens similar (L11–21 0.089 → 0.215); logit lens
roughly flat (0.29 → 0.31, 0.36 → 0.38, 0.19 → 0.20). Two readings, both
honest: (1) the junk is not an additive mean-activation artifact — consistent
with the multiplicative signature above; (2) part of the effect is
mechanical: centering removes the common function-word mass that crowded the
top-10, exposing whatever sits underneath. Either way, **"subtract the mean
and the junk goes away" is false on this model** — the simplest correction
story fails, which raises the stakes for step 7's score-space correction
(s − m_t), the version that needs no activation surgery. Note the
consistency with H1 as registered: phoenix predicted that subtracting the
prior *shifts readouts toward rarer tokens*, and that is behaviorally what
centering produced here — the same observation from two directions.

## 4c The H1 regression — H1 (frequency prior) SUPPORTED at mid-depth; the junk is a separate, frequency-independent component

Reported as the pre-committed range across three cells (D31), Spearman of m_t
vs log frequency per layer (`src.h1_regression` →
`results/step4_h1_regression.json` + `step4_h1_r_by_depth.png`; the script
refuses to run if the 4.0 gate recorded a FAIL):

| J-lens layer | Latin ∩ pile counts (n=92,858) | whole vocab, wordfreq (n=224,457) | non-Latin, wordfreq (n=94,437) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | −0.06 | +0.01 | +0.03 |
| 6 | +0.04 | +0.07 | +0.07 |
| 12 | +0.20 | +0.16 | +0.09 |
| 18 | **+0.48** | **+0.25** | **+0.14** |
| 24 | +0.44 | +0.20 | +0.09 |
| 30 | +0.18 | +0.09 | +0.03 |

- **Mid-depth (L5–L28): all three cells agree in sign — positive.** Higher
  mean score for more frequent tokens, a unigram-prior-like tilt, peaking
  around L18–21. Magnitude is strongly source-dependent (0.48 vs 0.25 vs
  0.14 at the peak) — the D31 range, exactly as the sources' 0.24 mutual
  agreement predicted. Consistent in sign with Route A's finding that W_U·β
  points toward frequent tokens.
- **Early layers (L0–4): the cells disagree in sign** (Latin negative,
  wordfreq cells ~zero/positive), and every |ρ| < 0.12. **Where the junk
  actually lives, frequency explains almost nothing, and what little it
  explains depends on which frequency you mean.** Per the pre-set decision
  rule this disagreement is reported as a finding, not smoothed over.
- **Verdict against the REGISTERED H1** (plan v2: *"final-layer bias
  correlates with log token-frequency at r≈0.67; subtracting it shifts
  readouts toward rarer tokens"* — i.e. the offset is a frequency prior,
  common tokens collect score regardless of context): **SUPPORTED.** Route A
  replicates the bias–frequency correlation (0.70/0.73 vs claimed 0.67); the
  measured m_t carries the same positive tilt at mid-depth in all three
  cells; and Route B's centering behaviorally reproduces the second half of
  the claim — remove the shared component and readouts shift toward rarer
  tokens (the junk fraction rises). What H1 does NOT do is reach the early
  layers: there the frequency structure vanishes, so the offset decomposes
  into at least two parts — a frequency prior growing toward mid-depth, and
  an early-layer junk component that frequency does not touch.
- **Interpretive limit, stated plainly:** a positive m_t–frequency
  correlation cannot by itself distinguish "instrument bias toward frequent
  tokens" from "frequent tokens are genuinely predicted often" (m_t contains
  both). The σ_t diagnostic and step 7's behavioral correction carry that
  weight.
- Bare-word dual-report (D33): restricting the wordfreq cells to
  `is_bare_word` shifts every Spearman by ≤ 0.05; the only sign flips occur
  where |ρ| < 0.02. A non-event, reported as one.
- **Standing caveat on every 4c number (D26–D28):** for non-Latin tokens no
  frequency measure from Qwen's own training data exists in this project;
  wordfreq fixes coverage, not provenance.

## Correction (21 Aug, same day — prompted by Ameya's recheck of the registered hypothesis)

The first committed version of this entry (4ea5c91/a155256) framed 4c as
"H1 as junk-explanation fails; H1 as frequency-prior holds," attributing to
H1 a prediction it never made — that the junk is rare tokens boosted by
default, which would require a *negative* frequency correlation concentrated
early. The registered H1 (`mats-application-plan-v2.md`, "The question") is
the **frequency prior itself**, phoenix's claim, and against that claim the
verdict is **supported at mid-depth**, with the registered caveats (D31
source-dependence; bias-vs-content attribution open on Qwen). The
"junk is not frequency-structured" result stands unchanged, but it is a new
finding about the offset's *composition*, not a refutation of H1.

Checked before this correction: no script encodes any directional
assumption (all battery statistics are sign-agnostic correlations; the 4.0
gate and the D31 sign-agreement check treat both signs symmetrically), and
no results file contains hypothesis text. **No number changed; nothing was
rerun because nothing computational depended on the framing.** The error
lived in this entry's prose, one steps.md phrase (also corrected), and the
conversation around it.
