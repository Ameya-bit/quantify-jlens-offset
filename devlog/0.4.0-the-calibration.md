# 0.4.0 — The calibration (step 7 complete)

*26 Aug 2026. Qwen3.5-4B. `src/calibration.py`: 138 forward passes (the
step-3 two-hop set), rank of the known intermediate at every layer × 3
instruments, raw vs corrected, at the last prompt position. Refuses to start
if the step-4.0 noise-null gate failed. Outputs:
`results/step7_calibration.json` + `step7_ranks.npz` +
`step7_rank_by_depth.png`.*

## What step 7 asked

The payoff question: the offset `m_t` is stable (4.0), partly
frequency-shaped (4c), partly recipe-specific transport junk (6) — so does
*subtracting it* make the lens better at reading content? Correction in
SCORE space only: `s_t(h) − m_t`, per instrument, per layer — the route left
standing after step 4b killed activation-space centering (nonlinear norm
path; centering made the junk worse).

Test bench: rank of the two-hop intermediate (Munich → " Germany") among all
248,320 tokens, 1 = top. Headline = the 72 strict capital/capital_of items
(D21); all 138 as robustness. Variants, registered in order (D41): raw;
corrected (primary); z-scored `(s−m_t)/σ_t` (exploratory secondary);
shuffled-m_t null (3 seeds).

**Gate (D41, registered 26 Aug before any code):** corrected beats raw on
median intermediate rank at a majority of L0–14 on J AND R, paired sign test
p<0.05 at ≥3 of those layers per instrument.

## Verdict: GATE FAIL — the registered correction makes the readout worse

Not marginally: the flat subtraction **significantly hurts** the
intermediate's rank on J at 27/31 layers (every layer from L4 up), on R at
25/31 (every layer from L6), and on the logit lens at L0–18. Where the
intermediate is actually being read out (L17–23), the damage is large:

| median rank, 72 strict items | L18 | L20 | L23 |
|---|---|---|---|
| J raw → corrected | 13,348 → 74,731 | 1,000 → 6,656 | 768 → 4,278 |
| R raw → corrected | 11,412 → 98,576 | 1,052 → 9,798 | 788 → 4,726 |

Gate arithmetic: J improves the median at 1/15 layers of L0–14 (needs 8+),
R at 5/15. Reported as registered; the gate is not renegotiated.

## Finding 1 — the offset is mostly signal at the depths that matter (P3, with force)

The registered prediction P3 said mid-depth correction "may hurt or do
nothing" because the mid-depth offset component is frequency-shaped and
shared across instruments (steps 4c/6) — plausibly the model's genuine
prior, not instrument error. It hurts, everywhere that the readout works.
The mechanism is visible in the design: " Germany" is exactly the kind of
frequent token the frequency prior boosts, so subtracting `m_t` subtracts
the content along with the "bias". The shuffled null separates this from
generic noise: shuffled-m_t correction lands near raw (J L20: raw 1,000,
shuffled 1,765, real corrected 6,656) — the damage is specific to the true
offset, i.e. the correction removes *real, token-identity-linked signal*,
not variance. This is the behavioral counterpart of 4c: the frequency
component of the offset is not removable bias; it is what the model
actually computes.

## Finding 2 — the narrow registered success: R's early junk band is correctable (P1/P2)

R is the one instrument with a coherent early improvement: corrected
sig-beats raw at exactly L0–4 (median L0 161,687 → 76,094, ≈2×; sign test
p<0.05 at all five layers). That is precisely the band where step 6 located
R's recipe-specific transport junk (R/J offset ratio 1.94, near-disjoint
token sets). J improves only at L2 — consistent with its early offset being
half R's size. P1 (helps most on R) and P2 (early, transported lenses)
are supported *in that band only*; the band is too narrow to pass the gate.
Removable instrument bias exists, and it lives exactly where step 6 said the
transport artefact lives — it just isn't most of the offset.

## Finding 3 — the late-layer regime: z-scoring rescues the junk rebound (exploratory)

From L24 the picture inverts. The flat correction helps the LOGIT lens at
L19–29 (intermediate L24: 2,678 → 1,274; answer token L24: 340 → 104), and
the z-scored variant sig-helps **all three** instruments late (J/R at L24,
L27–30; logit L20–30; answer token at logit L24: 340 → 20, J L27: 72 → 58).
Late is where steps 2/6 found the transported lenses' junk *rebound* (top-100
m_t junk share 0.61–0.70 at L27–28) — a junk-dominated band, and the only
band where dividing by σ_t (de-emphasizing high-variance content tokens)
pays. Labeled exploratory: the z variant was registered as secondary, and
its tiny-σ noise amplification caveat stands.

## Self-checks

- J₃₀=R₃₀=I: all three instruments agree at L30 within atol 1e-3 on every
  item (asserted in-run).
- Independent re-derivation: 27 rank cells (3 items × {L0, L20, L30} × 3
  instruments, raw + corrected) recomputed with throwaway code sharing no
  readout path — fresh forward pass, hand-rolled RMSNorm from the weight
  vector, fp64, sum-comparison rank. 25/27 exact, 2 off by exactly 1 rank
  out of ~1e5 (fp32-vs-fp64 near-ties). First attempt used `x̂·w` and
  mismatched everywhere — Qwen3.5's RMSNorm is `x̂·(1+w)` (zero-init
  weight); a useful reminder that the check was real.
- Determinism: full rerun end-to-end (fresh forward passes); all rank
  arrays bit-identical, JSON identical up to the added L30 note field.
- L30 top-1-is-answer = 21/138 is **informational, not a failed check**:
  the model has 32 blocks and lens target layer 30 is the PENULTIMATE
  block; the step-3 filter constrains block-31 output. The last block does
  most of the final answer promotion (L30 median intermediate rank 504,
  answer rank 14).

## Honest limits

- `m_t` is pile-estimated; the two-hop prompts are a different distribution.
  Genuine out-of-sample test, but the mid-depth *harm* is too large and too
  systematic to be a transfer artefact (a non-transferring offset would act
  like the shuffled null, which sits near raw).
- Ranks use 1 + #(strictly greater): ties flatter the target; measure-zero
  off the padded rows in fp32.
- n=72 headline items, 25 distinct intermediates — sign tests are paired
  per item but intermediates repeat across templates; item-level
  independence is partial. Full-138 medians agree in direction.
- The z-scored late-layer result is exploratory and untested elsewhere.

## What the writeup inherits (takeaway-map slot 5: "correctable?")

**Mostly no — and that is the payoff finding.** The stable offset is not,
at the depths where lenses are useful, an instrument artifact you can
subtract away: flat score-space correction damages the readout because the
offset's main mid-depth component is the model's own frequency-shaped
prior (Findings 1; corroborates 4c and step 6's "shared component"). What
IS correctable is exactly what steps 4–6 diagnosed as instrument-made: R's
early transport junk (Finding 2) and the transported lenses' late junk
rebound, the latter only via the variance-aware variant (Finding 3). The
practical advice for lens users writes itself: don't de-bias by
subtraction mid-stack; treat early-R and late-J/R readouts with per-token
offset statistics in hand.
