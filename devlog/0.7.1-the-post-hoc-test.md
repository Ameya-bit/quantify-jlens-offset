# 0.7.1 — The post-hoc significance test on the Gemma headline gap

*17 Sep 2026. No new experiments; a statistical test added to the D44
readout after the executive summary was drafted. Decision D45. Code:
src/taboo_analysis_gemma.py (`sign_flip_test`, `paired_test`). Evidence:

## Why this exists, and why it is labelled post-hoc

The 0.7.0 headline is a leave-one-word-out (LOO) accuracy of 0.805 for the
z-scored J-lens against 0.665 for their protocol (raw logit) on 20 words.
D43/D44 registered the *direction* predictions (P1, P2, P3) but never a
test of whether that 0.14 gap is distinguishable from noise at n = 20.
While the summary was being redlined the question came up, the numbers
were computed ad hoc from the per-word accuracies already in the JSON, and
this entry moves them into the repo so the summary cites nothing the
tracked code does not produce.

**This is a post-hoc test, not a registered one.** It was chosen after
the headline was known. It changes no verdict (P1–P3 stand as reported in
0.7.0); it qualifies how the headline gap should be read.

## The test

- **Unit:** the 20 held-out words; for each, the LOO accuracy of cell A
  minus the LOO accuracy of cell B (`loo_headline.<cell>.per_word[].accuracy`).
- **Statistic:** the mean paired difference.
- **Headline test:** exact sign-flip (permutation) test. Under the null of
  no difference each word's sign is a fair coin, so the null distribution is
  the statistic over all 2^20 = 1,048,576 sign assignments, enumerated in
  full (no Monte Carlo, so no seed). Two-sided: p = share of assignments
  whose |mean| ≥ the observed |mean|. Tied words contribute zero under every
  assignment, exactly as they do in the observed mean.
- **Cross-check:** Wilcoxon signed-rank (`scipy.stats.wilcoxon`, ties
  dropped, two-sided). Reported alongside; the sign-flip value is the
  headline because it uses the actual differences rather than their ranks.
- **Pairs:** zscore/J vs raw/logit (their protocol), and zscore/J vs
  zscore/logit (isolates the transport from the calibration).

## Result

| pair | mean diff | wins / losses / ties | sign-flip p | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| zscore/J vs raw/logit | +0.14 (0.805 − 0.665) | 9 / 6 / 5 | **0.193** | 0.155 |
| zscore/J vs zscore/logit | +0.09 (0.805 − 0.715) | 4 / 3 / 13 | **0.234** | 0.281 |

Independent check: `scipy.stats.permutation_test` with
`permutation_type="samples"`, `n_resamples=np.inf` gives 0.1929 and 0.2344,
identical to four decimals.

## What it means for the story

The 0.805 vs 0.665 headline is a **point estimate, not a significant
difference**. Thirteen of twenty words tie between z-scored J and z-scored
logit, so the J-vs-logit comparison rests on seven words. The registered
findings that survive untouched: calibration turns the J-lens from the
worst instrument into the best under LOO (P1 fails raw, passes calibrated),
and z-scoring beats raw on J across the whole late band (P2, five of five
layers). What this entry adds is that "calibrated J beats their protocol
by 14 points" must be read as "by 14 points, p ≈ 0.19, n = 20", and that
the gain may come from calibration rather than from the J-lens itself.
