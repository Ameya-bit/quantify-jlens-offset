# 0.0.2 — Reproducing the junk

**Date:** 20 Aug 2026 · **Covers steps.md Step 2** · **Commits:** `8dbd805`, `888ffad`, `e1788b1`, `3549402`, `4724e1f`, `30dd1bc`

## What this entry is about

The study exists to explain the **junk** — meaningless tokens (punctuation
runs, foreign-script fragments, broken byte pieces) that show up in lens
readouts even though they have nothing to do with the text. Before
explaining it, we must show it exists *on our setup*. That is Step 2's gate.

Second thread: the "does the bias explain it?" hypothesis needs a model
whose final rescaling step *has* a bias. None of the 8 released lenses is
for such a model, so we are fitting our own lens for **Pythia-1.4B** with
the authors' official tool, mirroring the released Qwen lens recipe
(same dataset, texts cut at 128 tokens, skip first 4 positions, destination
= second-to-last layer, 25 texts).

## Code added

- `src/fit_pythia.py` — wraps the official `jlens.fit` for Pythia-1.4B.
  `--smoke` mode fits on 1 text and writes timing projections. Full
  precision, weights frozen, checkpoint after every text. Commit `8dbd805`;
  fix in `888ffad` (our newer `transformers` renamed Pythia's output head
  `embed_out → lm_head`, so the official auto-locator missed it; we pass
  the component map explicitly — an official escape hatch, no code surgery).
- `src/flags.py` — junk flags for one decoded token: `punctuation`
  (no letters/digits), `byte_fragment` (contains `�`), `non_latin`
  (letters beyond Latin), `leading_space` (recorded, *not* junk). A token
  is junk if any of the first three fire. Self-check: 12 cases, passes.
  Known limits (documented in the file): obscene-but-well-formed English
  words are not flagged; non-Latin is junk only *as a readout for English
  text*. The same rule applies to every layer/lens, so comparisons are fair.
  Commit `3549402`.
- `src/junk_survey.py` — the evidence collector: 25 seeded pile-10k texts
  (rows 25–9999 only; rows 0–24 are the lens-fit corpus of both the
  released Qwen lens and our Pythia fit — reading out on the lens's own
  training texts would be circular), 5 positions each (never the first 4),
  all 31 lensed layers, all three instruments, top-10 tokens with flags →
  `results/step2_readouts.json` (11,625 readouts). Commit `4724e1f`.
- `src/step2_figures.py` — figures derived from the JSON only. Commit
  `4724e1f`, font/title fix in `30dd1bc`.

## Addendum (20 Aug, later the same day) — attacking the surprise

House rule: when a result surprises, the next hour goes to breaking it. The
reviewer (Ameya) challenged the "logit-lens is junkier than J/R at every
layer" reading against the workspace paper's statement that the lenses
agree closely. Outcome of the attack: **half the surprise did not survive.**

**No conflict with the paper existed in the first place.** The paper's
actual claim is that the lenses "agree closely in the model's *last several
layers* and diverge earlier"; Neel's review compares lenses with rank-based
and causal metrics. Neither measures junk content. Their rulers and ours
measure different things, so our junk numbers could never contradict their
agreement claims — and vice versa.

**We replicate their claim on their kind of ruler.** New figure
`results/step2_overlap_L30.png`: mean top-10 overlap with the same
position's layer-30 readout climbs from ~0/10 early to ~6/10 at layer 29
for all three instruments nearly in lockstep, diverging below — the paper's
picture, junk-proxy-free, on our rig.
> **CORRECTED by addendum 4 (breaks 3 and 4).** Two errors here. (a) "Nearly
> in lockstep" is false: at L18 the means are J 0.99 / R 1.12 / logit 0.42,
> and J/R run ~2× the logit lens from L12 to L25. (b) Overlap with the
> *destination* is not lens-vs-lens agreement, which is what the paper's
> claim is about. `step2_overlap_L30.png` is replaced by
> `step2_lens_agreement.png` (pairwise left, distance-to-destination right).

**The between-lens junk claim is RETRACTED as recorder bias.** Decomposition
(`results/step2_junk_composition.json`): the whole logit-vs-J gap sits in
one flag, `non_latin` (mid-layers: logit 3.6/10 vs J 0.8/10; punctuation
and byte-fragment counts are essentially equal). Meanwhile the J-lens's
characteristic junk — Latin word-fragments like 'oooo', 'fictiona',
'correcti' — passes every flag and is counted as clean. The proxy
systematically catches the logit-lens's junk style and misses the J-lens's,
so it cannot rank instruments. Figure titles now claim within-instrument
depth trends only.

**What still stands.** The gate: junk exists on our setup, in all three
instruments, fading with depth — existence is robust to an undercounting
detector. What Step 2 does NOT establish (by design): that the junk is a
stable offset (Step 4.0's null test), what it is made of (4a–4c), or which
instrument is cleaner (behavioral `m_t` comparison, Step 4/6).

Reproduce: `.venv/bin/python -m src.step2_figures` regenerates both new
artifacts from the committed JSON. Sources: transformer-circuits.pub/2026/
workspace ("Comparison to Related Techniques"); lesswrong.com post
zFJ3ZdQwrTWE9jT5S (Neel's review).

## Addendum 2 — Pythia fit landed

25/25 texts, 43.8 min, `results/pythia_jlens.pt` (22 matrices, layers
0–21, destination 22; *.pt gitignored — reproduce via
`.venv/bin/python -m src.fit_pythia --n-prompts 25`). Convergence stat fell
0.40 → 0.06 over the run; matrix scale stayed ~1.0–1.08 throughout.

**Sanity battery (src/sanity_pythia.py): 3/3 PASS** →
`results/pythia_sanity.json`. Widened same-day after review: the agreement
check now runs on the same 25 pile texts × 5 positions as the Qwen survey
(125 samples/layer, disjoint from both fit corpora), and the facts check
records the logit-lens's first-hit layer as a baseline.

(1) Wiring: logit-lens on the final block reproduces the model's true
next-token top-10 exactly. (2) Late-layer J~logit overlap at n=125:
1.2 / 2.6 / **5.02** at layers 19/20/21 (gate ≥5 at L21) — the original
3-sample estimate (5.0) held up almost exactly. (3) Known facts, J-lens
vs logit-lens first-hit layer: Paris L15 vs L15, Tokyo **L15 vs L17**,
oxygen L13 vs L13 — the fitted lens is never later than the baseline and
once 2 layers earlier: it adds (modest) value, and is at least not harmful.

For symmetry, the Qwen step-1 agreement check was retroactively upgraded
to the same standard with **zero new forward passes** (`src/qwen_agreement.py`
re-reads the committed survey JSON): at n=125, mean overlap 6.46/7.44 at
layers 28/29 (step-1 gate analog ≥5 ✓), layer 30 exactly 10/10 ✓ →
`results/qwen_agreement.json`. Step 1's 3-prompt verdict survives the
wide sample.

**Caveat, disclosed:** Pythia's late-layer agreement remains weaker than
Qwen's analog (5.0 at L21 vs 6.5–7.4 at L28/29). Note the baseline itself
is suspect on this family — the tuned-lens literature documents the logit
lens as unreliable on Pythia-class models — so low overlap does not
localize the fault to our fitted lens; the facts check (ground-truth-based)
is the stronger evidence it transports meaning. Step-4 Pythia results
inherit this caveat. Step 4 is cleared to use the lens.

## Addendum 3 (20 Aug) — R-lens scale check

Reviewer asked why our figures show R barely above J when the R-lens post
claims better early-layer faithfulness. Checked the post
(lesswrong.com/posts/nv8oedrnLXKRzNEL9): its evidence is pass@10 on
known-intermediate prompts + ablations, and it states there is **no R-lens
advantage for the smallest dense model tested** — and the smallest dense
model in its released collection (the same `workspace-lenses` repo our
lenses come from) is **qwen3.5-4b, our headline model**. So R≈J here is the
post's own predicted result. Its trash-token claim is explicitly
qualitative ("seems to contain drastically fewer"); our battery is the
first quantification. Two consequences recorded in steps.md Step 6: the
**weak-lever caveat** (J≈R at this scale makes "offset survives the J→R
swap" underdetermined; "shrinks" stays decisive) and the post-Step-4
**qwen3.5-9b swap-battery option** to strengthen the lever.

## Addendum 4 (20 Aug) — red team of step 2's takeaways before opening step 3

House rule again: before building on step 2, try to break it. Most of the
machinery survived; several of the *claims about it* did not.

**What survived, verified independently.** 300 cells (4 random rows × 5
positions × 5 layers × 3 instruments) were recomputed from scratch through a
fresh forward pass: token-for-token identical to the committed JSON, max
probability delta 5e-6. The whole survey was then re-run after the flag
change below: **0 token-list differences and 0 probability differences
across all 11,625 cells** — only the flag fields moved. Layer-30 identity
re-checked per cell (0 mismatches, prob delta exactly 0.0). Released-lens
`max|J₃₀ − I| = 0.0` for both J and R. The `≥5` late-agreement gate was
*not* set post-hoc: `MIN_MEAN_OVERLAP_28_29 = 5.0` is in `src/sanity.py`
from step 1. No position artifact: the three positions sampled in ≥22 texts
(4, 35, 66, 96, 127) all give 0.157–0.199 early-layer junk, so position 4's
proximity to the attention sink is not driving anything.

**Break 1 — the junk figure had no yardstick.** `results/step2_baselines.json`
(new, `src/null_baselines.py`) supplies three:

| reference | rate |
|---|---|
| uniform draw from Qwen's 248k vocabulary | **0.432** (43% of the vocabulary is non-Latin) |
| random-**rotation** lens, same norm→`W_U` path, 5 seeds | **0.386** (0.318–0.470; seed means 0.360–0.408) |
| the tokens the model is actually trying to predict | **0.000** |
| layer 30 (the model's own readout) | **0.022** |

This is step 8's pre-registered "shuffled-J / random-rotation null" pulled
forward, and it splits one question into two that have *opposite* answers:
*is the lens broken?* — no, it runs 3–6× below the null at every depth;
*does it read out tokens that cannot be right?* — yes, 20% early against a
corpus base rate of exactly 0. Step 2's gate is the second question. Five
seeds, not one, because a single draw ranges over 0.36–0.41.

**Break 2 — punctuation was being scored as junk, and it is a correct
prediction.** Real pile text is **15.0%** punctuation tokens, and four of the
corpus's ten commonest tokens (`,` `.` `\n\n` `\n`) are punctuation — so a
perfect unigram predictor scored 0.4 under the old rule. Worse, the two
components move in *opposite* directions through mid-depth (paired by text,
25 clusters, L10-13 → L16-19): J anomaly 0.112 → 0.075 (t = −3.49) while J
punctuation 0.033 → 0.100 (t = +4.02); R anomaly 0.127 → 0.070 (t = −4.80),
punctuation 0.023 → 0.084 (t = +5.44). **The mid-depth "plateau" in the
old figure was the sum of a real improvement and a second real improvement
scored with the wrong sign.** `is_junk` is now `byte_fragment | non_latin`
only; punctuation is plotted as its own series against the 0.150 text rate.
Scoping to "concept slots" (dividing by 10 − punctuation) was considered and
rejected — the denominator would shrink exactly where punctuation peaks,
re-inflating those layers ~12%. Fixed denominator keeps layers, instruments
and baselines comparable, and counting punctuation as clean makes the
reported rate a conservative lower bound.

**Break 3 — "nearly in lockstep" (this file, above) is wrong.** Overlap with
the layer-30 readout at L18 is J 0.99 / R 1.12 / **logit 0.42**; at L22,
2.02 / 2.22 / **1.38**. J and R run ~2× the logit lens from L12 to L25. That
*under*-claimed: it is junk-proxy-free evidence that the translation
matrices help at mid-depth — the thing the addendum-1 retraction had to give
up — and it was sitting in the committed JSON unread.

**Break 4 — that figure also measured the wrong thing.** Overlap with the
*destination* is not lens-vs-lens agreement (two lenses can each share 6
tokens with layer 30 and share none with each other), yet it was titled as
replicating the paper's agreement claim. `step2_overlap_L30.png` is replaced
by `step2_lens_agreement.png`, which plots pairwise agreement on the left and
keeps distance-to-destination on the right, correctly labelled.

**Break 5 — "J and R are nearly identical to each other throughout" (this
file, above) is FALSE below layer 27.** Pairwise top-10 overlap: **0.35/10 at
L0**, 4.72 at L12, 6.64 at L20, and only ≥8.7 from L27. They are near-twins
at the destination and substantially different instruments exactly where the
junk lives. Recorded in steps.md Step 6: this makes the J→R swap lever
*stronger* than the weak-lever caveat assumed, so the qwen3.5-9b download may
not be needed.

**Break 6 (minor).** The old figure subtitle called 3,875 "prompt-position
pairs"; that is readouts per instrument. There are **125** pairs × 31 layers.
Fixed. Also: step 1 compared top-10 by token *id*, while `qwen_agreement.py`
and the figures compare decoded *strings*; 15 of 11,625 cells contain a
duplicate decoded string, so the discrepancy is ~0.1% — noted, not chased.

**Left standing, deliberately.** The circularity argument is weaker than
stated: the released lens's provenance gives `n_prompts=25, docs_consumed=25,
dataset=pile-10k` but **no row indices** (`git_commit: "modal"`), so
"rows 0–24 are its fit corpus" is an inference, not a fact. Only our Pythia
fit is verifiably `texts[:25]` (`fit_pythia.py`). Excluding rows 0–24 is free
insurance and the residual risk is ~6% of any overlap at all; the wording is
softened rather than the experiment redone.

**A finding, banked for step 6 rather than chased now.** On the corrected
junk rule, J and R read out *more* impossible tokens at L24-27 than at
L18-21 — J 0.069 → 0.109 (t = +5.01, 22/25 texts), R 0.065 → 0.094
(t = +4.05, 20/25) — while the logit lens moves monotonically the other way,
0.369 → 0.216 (t = −10.90, **0/25** texts). A late anomaly rebound that
appears only in the *translated* instruments is a transport-error signature,
i.e. H3 evidence. Visible in the left panel of the new figure; not analysed
further here because that is step 6's job.

**Net effect on the gate: unchanged, better defended.** Junk exists on our
setup, in all three instruments, at a rate the corpus says is impossible —
now stated against a null instead of in a vacuum. The corrected shape is not
"high early, fading": the logit lens *rises* from 0.134 at L0 to 0.394 at
L19 before clearing, while J/R fall early, rebound at L22-26, and clear only
in the last ~4 layers. Figure titles updated to say that.
