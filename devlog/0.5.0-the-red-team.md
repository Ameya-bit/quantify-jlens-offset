## 8d — the step-7 headline survives its sharpest attack (prediction holds)

Registered prediction: if subtracting m_t hurts because the frequency
component is real signal, damage = log2(rank_corrected/rank_raw) should
scale with the intermediate token's frequency. It does, with a
dose-response curve: at working depths L17–23, Spearman rho(zipf, damage)
over the 25 distinct intermediates = **0.685 (J)** and **0.614 (R)**,
p < 0.005 (all-138 slice: 0.716 / 0.674). The logit lens, which has no
transported offset, shows no relationship (rho 0.047) — exactly as the
mechanism requires. Break criterion (rare half helped by correction) did
NOT trigger: even " Morocco"/" Norway"-grade intermediates are hurt
(+1.5 bits median on J). Early band L0–4 shows no positive slope
(J −0.17, R −0.32), consistent with the early offset being transport junk,
not frequency.

Checks: independent hand-rolled rank correlation 0.681 vs script 0.685
(tie handling); disclosure: the headline-72 subset covers 21/25
intermediates, so its registered "bottom 12" rare half is 11 there; the
all-138 slice uses all 25 and agrees.

## 8a — GATE FAIL: m_t is not context-independent across domains

Gate (D42): Pearson(m_t) ≥ 0.8 between every domain pair at ≥16/31 layers,
J-lens. Result: **0 of 6 pairs pass** (layers passing: CC~Wiki 21,
PubMed~Wiki 13, CC~PubMed 9, CC~Github 6, Github~PubMed 5, Github~Wiki 5).
Not noise: every arm's own split-half ceiling is ≥ 0.947 (median ≈ 0.97–0.99),
while cross-domain medians run 0.67–0.87. The registered consequence
applies as written: **the claim narrows to "within-distribution offset."**
The gate is reported as registered, not renegotiated.

The failure is structured, and it corroborates H1: the worst layers are
L9–13 for every single pair — precisely the mid-depth band where 4c located
the frequency component. Domains have different token frequencies; a
frequency-shaped offset must be domain-dependent there. Github (the most
alien token distribution) is in all three worst pairs and adds an L30 dip.

## 8b — GATE PASS, maximally: the third disjoint sample replicates m_t

Fresh mixed 100-doc sample (seed 3, fully disjoint): Pearson vs the
original step-4 m_t ≥ 0.8 at **31/31 layers** (min 0.981, median 0.988),
J-lens. Within-distribution, the offset replicates essentially perfectly on
a third estimate. Together with 8a: m_t is a stable property of
(lens, layer, text distribution) — not of the lens alone.

## 8c — the nulls: stability and size are generic; the *direction* is not

Registered comparative question (no gate): does an arbitrary map through
the same final-norm → W_U path produce an offset as stable and as big as
the fitted J? **Yes on both counts, and then some** (mixed arm, split-half
Pearson / D39 size):

| kind | split-half med | D39 size med |
|---|---|---|
| J | 0.970 | 2.47 |
| R | 0.986 | 3.42 |
| logit | 0.972 | 1.95 |
| rot (random rotation, seed 0) | 0.990 | 3.90 |
| shufJ (J from layer+15 mod 31) | 0.972 | 2.22 |

So the step-4.0 result "a stable offset exists" is, by itself, NOT evidence
about the lens: any linear readout through norm → W_U yields a stable,
sizable m_t. What the rotation null does NOT reproduce is the offset's
direction: Pearson(rot m_t, J m_t) ≈ 0 at every probed layer (−0.03…0.13),
while shufJ~J reaches 0.63 at L12 — the mid-depth frequency component shows
through any *fitted J* matrix but not through an arbitrary rotation. The
content characterization (4c frequency, step-6 transport junk, step-7
behavioral cost) is untouched; the stability claim alone is demoted from
"property of the lens" to "property of the readout path."
