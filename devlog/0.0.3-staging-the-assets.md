# 0.0.3 — Staging the assets

**Date:** 20–21 Aug 2026 · **Covers steps.md Step 3** · **Commits:** `fbf2207`, `1d8d637`, `8d9a70b`, `aacea46`, `2c13799`, `0ec89b0`, `448a8cb`

## What this entry is about

Step 3 buys no results. It gathers the three raw materials the later steps
need, and its whole value is in knowing what each one can and cannot support
*before* Step 4 leans on it. Every asset here shipped with a measured
limitation rather than a hopeful one, and two of them changed shape once
measured.

The three materials:

- **`log f_t`** — how common each token is. Hypothesis 1 says the junk in the
  readouts is really just common tokens showing up because they are common,
  so H1 cannot be tested without this.
- **Two-hop prompts** — questions needing two steps of reasoning, where we
  know the middle step. Step 7 checks whether correcting the offset makes
  that middle step visible earlier.
- **Δ_t** — which tokens the tuned model holds back relative to the raw one.
  Hypothesis 2's axis.

## Asset 1 — token frequency, and the hole in it

`src/frequencies.py` counts every token of all 10,000 pile-10k documents
under the Qwen, Pythia and GPT-2 tokenizers (15.6M / 15.4M / 17.4M tokens →
`results/step3_frequencies.json`, `results/step3_token_counts.npz`). Top-15
under each is `,` `.` ` the` ` of` ` and`, which is the sanity check.

Also done here in two lines: **Qwen instruct and base share an identical
tokenizer** (vocab dicts equal; a probe string with CJK, accents and
punctuation encodes identically). Δ_t therefore needs no fallback token list.

**Finding 1 — one document is 6.3% of the corpus.** A single pile-10k
document is 981k tokens. A unigram prior one document can move by 6% is a
sampling accident, so counts are kept twice: `full`, and `capped` at 8,192
tokens per document (339 documents exceed it). Capped is primary. The two
agree at only r = 0.977 in log space, so the choice is not cosmetic (D17).

**Finding 2 — pile-10k cannot see most of Qwen's vocabulary.** Only **42.8%**
of Qwen's 248,077 tokens appear at all, and only **9.3%** of its non-Latin
tokens. The Pile is English; Qwen's vocabulary is not. Tokens with no count
have no `log f_t` and drop out of the H1 regression — and the tokens that
drop out are exactly the CJK junk the study is about.

Measured rather than assumed: the *vocabulary* figure is not the *regression*
figure. Of tokens that actually appear in the step-2 readouts, only **11.1%**
(J-lens) lacked a frequency — 21.7% at layers 0–5, 6.0% mid-depth, worst case
41.0% for the logit lens at L14–21. Bad, not fatal, and stated per number
rather than hidden (D18).

## Asset 1b — two attempts to close that hole

**Attempt 1: Qwen's own tokenizer (`src/zipf_frequency.py`).** Ameya's
proposal, and a published method — a BPE tokenizer merges the most frequent
pair first, so its merge order is a frequency ordering computed on the
model's *own* corpus, which is the one thing pile-10k can never be
(arXiv:2407.16607, NeurIPS 2024; arXiv:2508.17771 does exactly this for
Chinese tokens). Qwen ships 247,587 ordered merges, so it is directly
readable. No Zipf calibration was performed, deliberately: frequency ∝
1/rank^s means −log(rank) is already a log-frequency proxy and `s` only
rescales the slope, which cannot change a Spearman ρ, an R², or a p-value.

**It does not work where we need it, and we checked before relying on it.**

| region | ρ vs pile count |
|---|---|
| overall | −0.676 |
| ids 0–10k | −0.377 |
| ids 50k–100k | −0.202 |
| ids 100k–150k | **+0.041** |
| ids 150k–200k | **+0.012** |
| non-Latin tokens | **−0.260** |

The healthy global figure is carried almost entirely by *between*-band
differences. Within a band it collapses; past id 100k — where the non-Latin
vocabulary lives — it is indistinguishable from zero and the sign flips.
Merge rank sorts tokens into coarse frequency tiers and says little within a
tier. Kept as a documented negative result, dropped from the working set
(D26, D32).

Free by-product: merge rank and raw token id agree to four decimals against
pile counts (−0.6762 vs −0.6766). **Qwen's token ids *are* merge order**, so
the principled construction bought nothing over the crude proxy.

**Attempt 2: `wordfreq` (`src/multilingual_freq.py`).** A frequency table
over 42 languages from Wikipedia, Books, Reddit, Twitter, OpenSubtitles,
SUBTLEX and Leeds. Max Zipf score over 12 languages per token — Qwen is
multilingual and its mixture is undisclosed, so "how common is this string in
whichever language uses it" is the closest available stand-in, and taking the
max avoids inventing mixture weights.

**This one works.**

| group | pile-10k | wordfreq | either |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latin, clean | 0.683 | 0.953 | 0.967 |
| **non-Latin** | **0.094** | **0.882** | **0.887** |
| punctuation | 0.685 | 0.084 | 0.722 |

Operationally: of the J-lens readout tokens pile-10k cannot measure,
**93.2% are rescued**, taking the unmeasurable share from 11.2% to **0.8%**.
H1 is testable on the non-Latin subpopulation for the first time.

Face validity is the strongest evidence: the top-scoring non-Latin tokens
come out as `的`, `の`, `в`, `के`, `في`, `है`, `に`, `이` — the highest-frequency
function words of Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Hindi, Arabic and Korean.

**Its own flaw, sized not described.** wordfreq strips punctuation and scores
the word left behind, so `.Scene` is scored as "scene", `.cpu` as "cpu",
`_exchange` as "exchange" — an over-estimate of the token's own frequency.
**8.9%** of scored tokens are affected, and **4.5%** of J-lens readout tokens
(R 5.9%, logit 5.9%). `is_bare_word` is now stored per token in
`results/step3_wordfreq.npz` so Step 4c can exclude them or report both ways
(D33).

## Asset 2 — two-hop prompts

`src/twohop.py` → `results/step3_twohop.json`. 45 hand-checked entities
(cities and landmarks → country → capital / currency / language) × 4
templates = 164 candidates, after requiring both the intermediate and the
answer to be a single Qwen token. Kept only where Qwen's true final-layer
top-1 next token is the answer: **138 kept**, 25 distinct intermediates. The
intermediate is never named in the prompt.

**The filter fact-checks us.** Its rejects are cases where the model is right
and our label was too narrow: Shanghai → "Mandarin" not "Chinese", Mumbai →
"New Delhi" not "Delhi", Toronto → "Canadian" not "dollar". It cannot catch a
label wrong in the same way the model is wrong, so ambiguous rows were
excluded at construction: no multi-capital countries, no contested "country"
(Scotland vs UK), no multilingual states in the language template (D22).

**We caught our own set cheating.** 70% of the currency answers are ` euro`,
so a model could ignore the city entirely and still score well. Every
template was re-run **blinded** — entity removed, nothing else changed:

| template | kept | shortcut (blinded top-1) | shortcut (blinded top-5) | strict two-hop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| capital | 39 | 0 | 0 | **39** |
| capital_of | 33 | 0 | 0 | **33** |
| language | 39 | 5 | 19 | 20 |
| currency | 27 | 0 | 20 | 7 |

The lenient criterion nearly missed it — currency's blinded top-1 is a
markdown artefact (` **`) while ` euro` sits at rank 2. Both criteria are
recorded and neither is applied in the file.

**Official Step 7 set: the 72 `capital` + `capital_of` items**, clean by both
criteria. The 66 contaminated items stay in the JSON with their flags rather
than being deleted, so a reader who thinks the blinding test was too strict
can check (D21). Diagnosis worth carrying: templating did not cause the
contamination — a **small answer space** did. There are only about six
currencies in play, so guessing works. More varied wording would not fix
that; more varied *answers* would.

## Asset 3 — Δ_t, and what it actually measures

`src/delta_t.py` → `results/step3_delta_t.json`, `.npz`. For 200 pile
documents (seed 1, disjoint from the step-2 survey's seed 0), at every
position from the 5th onward, both Qwen models emit a score for all 248,320
tokens; those become log-probabilities and are subtracted, then averaged over
23,912 samples. Positive = the raw model wants it more than the tuned model.

The two easy misreadings, both now in the module docstring: it uses **every**
position, not just the last; and it scores **every** token in the vocabulary
at each position, not just the token that actually came next. The real
continuation is never consulted — this measures what the models *want*, not
whether either is right.

**Finding 1 — Δ_t is frequency-contaminated.** Spearman(log f_t, Δ_t) =
**+0.354**, with median Δ rising monotonically across frequency quintiles
(−0.047, −0.033, +0.001, +0.045, +0.107). The tuned model shifts mass onto
chat and formatting tokens — all 25 most-*promoted* tokens are whitespace
variants or `<|im_end|>` — so ordinary content tokens are deflated in
proportion to the mass they held, and **common words look suppressed with no
suppression involved**. Step 5's frequency-matched control was already in the
plan; this makes it load-bearing rather than a refinement (D23).

**Finding 2 — the axis is style, not rudeness.** Ranked by Δ_t the top of the
vocabulary is ` ordinarily`, ` thankfully`, ` presently`, ` admittedly`,
` firstly`, ` anyways`, ` hubby`, ` thru`, ` seper`, plus scraped-text debris
(` ;-)`, `…the`). Informal, archaic, misspelled English. The dominant axis is
**"raw web text style" vs "clean assistant style."** Profanity is real but
secondary — top 0.3–4% of the vocabulary.

H2 was renamed accordingly, from "RLHF suppression" to **"tuning-induced
suppression"** (D30). Ameya's judgement, recorded as his: the general claim is
still worth making, since these are demonstrably inhibited tokens that the
J-lens surfaces as top contenders, and it no longer depends on a contested
account of what RLHF does internally. The cost he accepted is that the
general version sits *closer* to H1 — "informal / misspelled / web-debris" is
nearly a synonym for "rare" — so the frequency-matched control carries more
weight, not less. Honest headline: *tuning-inhibited tokens are
over-represented early, **over and above what their rarity predicts***.

Second limit: the base model's own pretraining data was filtered, so Δ_t sees
only tuning-stage suppression, not suppression from data filtering. The
successor experiment that would settle it — finetune a model to suppress a
target you chose, then rerun the battery, giving ground truth by construction
— is Ameya's, and belongs in the writeup rather than this project.

## Two failed gates, both rewritten, both recorded

Twice this step a pre-registered pass/fail test failed and was replaced. Both
are logged in full with their failing numbers (D24, D28) because *"the test
failed but the test was wrong"* is exactly what motivated reasoning sounds
like, and a reader has to be able to check.

**Gate 1 (Δ_t):** promised that profanity would score above neutral words.
Result 0.982 vs 0.953 — effectively tied, **FAIL**. The probe sets were not
frequency-matched: profanity median log f 3.37, neutral 6.43, so the controls
were ~20× more common and carried a larger frequency-driven Δ by Finding 1
above. Rewritten to compare each probe against clean, non-special tokens
within ±0.25 log-count (pool 92,846): **profanity excess +0.226 vs neutral
+0.052, a 4.4× separation, PASS.** Three of twelve profanity probes are still
not elevated (` slut`, ` retard`, ` whore`, the rarest of the set).

**Gate 2 (wordfreq):** promised that wordfreq would agree with pile counts on
non-Latin tokens. Result ρ = 0.245, **FAIL**. The gate was close to circular —
it asks whether a multilingual source agrees with an English-only source about
non-English tokens, and had it passed, wordfreq would have been adding
nothing. `的` appears 86 times in pile-10k and scores Zipf 7.79, because it is
the commonest character in Chinese and the Pile is not Chinese. Replaced with
three non-circular tests: face validity on non-Latin, Latin-only agreement
with the noisy tail trimmed (ρ +0.52 at count ≥1000, +0.39 at ≥100), and
readout coverage.

**The pattern, not the incidents.** Both gates were built against a reference
broken *in the specific region the asset targets*, though fine on average.
Both flaws were documented before the failure — Gate 2's in the same file's
docstring, Gate 1's in steps.md Step 5 from 19 Aug (*"Obscene tokens are also
rare — without the control, H1 and H2 are confounded"*) — so the reasoning does
not depend on the outcome. **Process change (Ameya, 21 Aug): a failed gate is
never rewritten unilaterally again. Report the failure, propose the
replacement, wait for input.**

## What Step 4 inherits

Sufficiency was measured, not assumed:

- **Coverage: solved.** 92.8% of the vocabulary has a frequency from at least one source, up from 42.8%.
- **Statistical power: not the bottleneck.** With 10k–130k tokens per group, correlations as small as |ρ| = 0.01 are detectable at p < 0.01. Whatever limits this study, it is not sample size.
- **Spread: adequate everywhere** (sd 0.9–2.3 per group), so no group is too flat for a relationship to show.
- **The real risk is validity, not power.** The two usable sources agree at only ρ ≈ 0.24, so H1's *magnitude* is source-dependent.

**Consequence for Step 4c, fixed now (D31): H1 is reported as a range across
three cells, never as a single number.**

| cell | source | why |
|---|---|---|
| Latin tokens | pile counts | best-measured; where the sources agree best (ρ 0.39–0.52 once the noisy tail is trimmed) |
| whole vocabulary | wordfreq | widest coverage on one consistent scale |
| **non-Latin only** | wordfreq | the junk itself, testable for the first time |

Agreement in sign across all three ⇒ H1 is robust, stated with more
confidence than one number would earn. Disagreement ⇒ that *is* the finding:
whether the offset tracks frequency depends on which frequency you mean.

**The hole that stays open and will not close in this project:** for non-Latin
tokens there is no frequency measure from Qwen's own training data. wordfreq
fixes coverage, not provenance; merge rank has the provenance but no signal
in that region. Written into steps.md so Step 4c inherits it rather than
rediscovering it.

**Reproduce everything in this entry:**

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m src.frequencies         # ~3 min  -> counts under 3 tokenizers
.venv/bin/python -m src.zipf_frequency      # ~2 min  -> merge-rank negative result
.venv/bin/python -m src.multilingual_freq   # ~2 min  -> wordfreq coverage + flaw
.venv/bin/python -m src.twohop              # ~3 min  -> 138 items, 72 clean
.venv/bin/python -m src.delta_t             # ~12 min -> both models; --from-cache to re-analyse only
```
